Amplifiers, Speakers, and Headphones

    Choose your speakers first and then choose an amplifier to match. For best sound quality, the amp should put out 10% more power than what the speakers are rated for.

    While speakers that enhance bass and mid-tones make music sound really good, this is not what you want for your home recording projects. Studio monitors should have a flat frequency response. This means that they do not favor certain frequencies over others. If your speakers are designed to enhance certain tones, then creating a mix that sounds good on other sound systems will be very difficult, if not impossible.

    Don't worry, flat frequency response speakers will still produce those fatter tones we all love. They just don't do it on their own. You'll need to use an equalizer. And they'll sound great!

    The same applies to headphones. A good pair of studio grade headphones won't be cheap compared to what you may be used to spending on ear buds. But it is essential that your headphones reproduce the music as it really sounds. Your headphones are going to connect to your audio interface. Keep in mind when choosing headphones, that for best sound quality, you want to be sure they match the impedance of the interface they will be plugged into. Check the manual from your intreface for this information and then choose headphones that match. Higher impedance headphones will be more expensive than their lower impedance counter parts.
