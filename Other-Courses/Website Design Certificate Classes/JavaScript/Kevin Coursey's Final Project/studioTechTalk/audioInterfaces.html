What is an audio interface?

    The audio interface is essential to the setup. It accepts analog audio input from sources like guitars, drums, keyboards, micorphones, etc and converts it to a digital format that your computer can understand. Those converted signals are then transferred via USB to your computer, where they are recorded or processed. The interface also converts digital signals from your computer back to analog during playback. Audio interfaces offer standard input options like 1/4" TSR or XLR. Many also offer MIDI capabilites and have one or two headphone outputs. Learn more

    How To Use It

    Audio interfaces come with numerous places to connect inputs, outputs, headphones, MIDI, and USB. Your instruments get plugged into the inputs. Your speakers get connected to the outputs. Any legacy MIDI equipment can be plugged into the MIDI ports. Most MIDI devices these days use USB. Lastly, the interface will connect to your computer via USB (likely USB3).

    Setup Information

    When buying an audio inferface, be sure that it is 'Class Compliant'. This means that the device is 'plug and play', meaning it will work without having to install proprietary drivers.
